Reads.

A working list of books that have shaped how I think. Not a canon. I'll add and remove as I change my mind.

On AI and alignment

  • If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies Yudkowsky & Soares

    The strongest version of the loss-of-control argument, stated plainly. You don't have to agree with every claim to think it's worth understanding.

  • Human Compatible Stuart Russell

    The academic-mainstream case for why we should worry about objectives, from one of the authors of the field's standard textbook.

  • The Alignment Problem Brian Christian

    A journalist's tour of the field. The best entry point for someone who has never read anything about alignment before.

  • Superintelligence Nick Bostrom

    The book that got me thinking about this seriously. Parts have aged; the core arguments haven't.

  • AI 2027 Kokotajlo et al.

    A detailed scenario of how the next few years could play out. Not a prediction — an existence proof that the path from here to loss-of-control is short.

How to think about the long run

  • What We Owe the Future William MacAskill

    The case that the long-term matters morally, and that we are at a time when our choices have outsized leverage.

  • The Precipice Toby Ord

    A careful accounting of existential risks — natural, anthropogenic, and technological — and what to do about each.

  • Doing Good Better William MacAskill

    How to think clearly about where your effort goes. Less about AI, more about the mental moves behind caring effectively.

On thinking clearly

  • Thinking, Fast and Slow Daniel Kahneman

    A working map of how human reasoning fails. Useful for the same reason interpretability is useful — you can't fix what you can't see.

  • The Scout Mindset Julia Galef

    On the difference between defending what you already believe and trying to see clearly. The single most underrated intellectual virtue.

  • How to Solve It George Pólya

    Old, short, still the best book on problem-solving I have read. Written for mathematics; applies everywhere.
